## 1. The Problem and Why AI Is Needed

A workstation (desk with monitor, lamp, and peripherals) wastes energy when left unattended. Traditional occupancy sensors have well-known limitations:
- **PIR (passive infrared):** only sensitive to motion within line of sight; they cut power when the user sits still reading and false-trigger when someone walks past the room.
- **Cameras:** invasive for privacy in work and home environments, with high computational and energy costs.

### The proposed solution
The system uses an **Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense** board in contact with the desk surface as a seismic/vibration pickup. Through the triaxial LSM9DS1 accelerometer, it detects elastic waves conducted by the work surface and generated by normal human activities (**typing on a keyboard**, **writing with a pen**, **handling objects/mouse**), automatically managing the power to the load.

### Why a simple energy threshold fails
A simple accelerometer RMS threshold (**energy threshold**) cannot distinguish between:
1. **Sustained low-intensity work** (e.g., writing with a pen or light typing on a membrane keyboard), which generates modest vibration energy;
2. **Transient events and incidental impacts** (an accidental bump to the desk, a door slamming, heavy footsteps nearby), which produce high energy peaks.

A fixed threshold incurs an inevitable trade-off: either it is too high (missing writing and cutting the light on the user) or too low (re-activating the load at every random bump).

The neural model (**MLP**), analyzing 33 statistical and temporal features, learns the dynamic signatures of activities and explicitly separates the reject class `incidental` from real activity.

## 2. System Architecture

### Activity Classes and Decision Policy
The system models 5 operational states:
- `0: idle` — Desk at rest (background noise, no contact).
- `1: typing` — Typing on a keyboard (rhythmic series of micro-impacts).
- `2: writing` — Writing on paper with a pen (continuous friction, characteristic frequencies).
- `3: handling` — Handling objects (placing a cup, moving a mouse, resting elbows).
- `4: incidental` — **Reject class**: single bump, accidental knock, vibration from external transit.

**Decision mapping:**
- $\{ \text{typing}, \text{writing}, \text{handling} \} \implies$ **Activity present**
- $\{ \text{idle}, \text{incidental} \} \implies$ **No activity**

An event classified as `incidental` **does not re-activate the load**, demonstrating the classifier's effectiveness over a trivial threshold.

### State Machine (FSM)
1. **Power-on debounce:** `ACT_CONSECUTIVE = 2` consecutive confirmed activity windows are required before switching the load to `POWER: ON`.
2. **Confidence filter:** If the winning class softmax score is $< \text{ACT\_THRESHOLD}$ (0.50), the window is treated as `idle`.
3. **Inactivity timeout:** If no activity is detected for $\Delta t > \text{IDLE\_TIMEOUT\_MS}$ (12 s in the demo firmware for easy testing; typically 5–15 minutes in production), the load is switched to `POWER: OFF`.
4. **Sniffing hysteresis:** When load is OFF, the system requires 3 consecutive quiet windows (`SNIFF_FALLBACK_WIN = 3`) before returning to low-power sniffing, preventing state chattering during natural typing pauses.
5. **Visual feedback:** Green LED on when the load is active; red LED on when power is cut.

## 3. Feature Extraction (33 Features)

Each window of 119 samples (~1 s at 119 Hz) is analyzed across 4 channels:
- X-axis acceleration: $a_X$
- Y-axis acceleration: $a_Y$
- Z-axis acceleration: $a_Z$
- Magnitude: $\text{mag} = \sqrt{a_X^2 + a_Y^2 + a_Z^2}$

For each of the 4 channels, **8 window statistics** are extracted:
1. **Mean** ($\mu$)
2. **Standard deviation** ($\sigma$)
3. **RMS** ($\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum x^2}$)
4. **Minimum** ($\min(x)$)
5. **Maximum** ($\max(x)$)
6. **Range** ($\max(x) - \min(x)$)
7. **Mean absolute value** ($\frac{1}{N}\sum |x|$)
8. **Zero-Crossing Rate (ZCR):** rate of zero crossings on the mean-removed signal.

One domain-specific feature is added on the magnitude channel:
- **Temporal Burstiness:** standard deviation of RMS values computed over ~10 non-overlapping sub-windows of 12 samples each (~100 ms). Separates the intermittent impacts of typing from the continuous friction of writing.

$$\text{Total Features} = 8 \times 4 + 1 = 33 \text{ features}$$

> **Theoretical note on PSD removal:**  
> By Parseval's theorem, the integral/mean of the spectral PSD equals $N \cdot \text{RMS}^2$. Computing a brute-force $O(N^2)$ DFT with $N=119$ would have required over 56,000 trigonometric calls per channel with no informational gain over the already-extracted RMS. Removing the PSD reduced computational latency while preserving the expressiveness of the feature vector.

## 4. Neural Model & On-Device Inference

- **Architecture:** Multilayer Perceptron (**MLP**):  
  $\text{Input (33)} \longrightarrow \text{Dense (12, ReLU)} \longrightarrow \text{Dense (6, ReLU)} \longrightarrow \text{Dense (5, Softmax)}$
- **Total parameters:** 521 float weights (~2 KB of Flash memory).
- **Inference engine:** Hand-coded C++ dense-matrix implementation in `inference.cpp` with Z-Score normalization `features_normalize()` and numerically stable softmax, avoiding the overhead and runtime arena of external libraries.

## 5. Low-Power Strategy & Energy Accounting

1. **Dual-Regime Duty-Cycled Sniffing (4 Hz):** When the desk is silent and load is `POWER: OFF`, the MCU sleeps in System ON Idle, waking only once every 250 ms (`SNIFF_INTERVAL_MS = 250`) to perform a single fast I²C sample read ($< 0.2\text{ ms}$). CPU active duty cycle drops below $0.1\%$. If sample acceleration delta $\ge \text{WAKE\_DELTA\_G}$ ($0.0060\,g$), the board transitions immediately to active sensing.
2. **Calibrated Noise Gate:** Once active, the standard deviation of the magnitude (effective vibration without gravity) is checked quickly. If $\text{RMS} < \text{GATE\_RMS}$ ($0.0015\,g$), the board skips inference and sleeps via `power_sleep()`, filtering baseline stationary noise ($< 0.0012\,g$) while letting light typing vibrations ($> 0.0017\,g$) reach the classifier.
3. **Anti-Chattering Hysteresis:** If the load is cut, the board remains in continuous active sensing for 3 consecutive quiet windows (`SNIFF_FALLBACK_WIN = 3`) before falling back to sniffing, ensuring natural pauses between keystrokes do not reset activity tracking.
4. **Duty cycling:** Microsecond-by-microsecond tracking with `micros()` of active, sleep, and sniffing states.
5. **Energy model (est):**
   - USB voltage: $V_{\text{BUS}} = 5.2\,\text{V}$ (measured with a USB meter, as in `EnergyAwarePath`)
   - Active current: $I_{\text{ACTIVE}} = 32.0\,\text{mA}$
   - Light sleep current: $I_{\text{SLEEP}} = 300\,\mu\text{A}$
   - Estimated load power: $P_{\text{LOAD}} = 50\,\text{W}$ (monitor, LED lamp, peripherals)
   - Energy saved on the load: $E_{\text{SAVED}} = P_{\text{LOAD}} \times t_{\text{OFF}}$ (tagged with `(est)`).

## 7. Operating Modes (Serial Commands at 115200 Baud)

- **`r` (Record):** Waits 1 second of pre-delay to allow the motion to start, acquires 1 window (119 samples), and transmits it over serial formatted as CSV between `START` and `END` markers.
- **`c` (Classify Single):** Acquires a single window, checks the `GATE_RMS` threshold, extracts features, runs inference, records latency, and prints the winning class and softmax scores for all 5 classes.
- **`m` (Monitor Continuous):** Toggles the autonomous operating mode. Cyclically acquires, applies the gate logic, runs inference, drives the state machine and power LED, and prints `POWER: ON` and `POWER: OFF` transitions.
- **`s` (Summary):** Prints the energy and time report for the session (active/sleep time, duty cycle, estimated board energy, load ON/OFF time, estimated energy saved on the load, and inference latency).
- **`q` (Quit):** Stops monitor mode.

## 8. User Guide and Demo

### Phase 1: Data Collection
Connect the Arduino via USB and run for each class:
```bash
python3 record_serial.py --port /dev/cu.usbmodemXXXX --label idle
python3 record_serial.py --port /dev/cu.usbmodemXXXX --label typing
python3 record_serial.py --port /dev/cu.usbmodemXXXX --label writing
python3 record_serial.py --port /dev/cu.usbmodemXXXX --label handling
python3 record_serial.py --port /dev/cu.usbmodemXXXX --label incidental
```
*Tip:* For `incidental`, collect single bumps, moderate knocks with the hand on different spots of the desk, and chair movements.

### Phase 2: Training and Model Export
Open and run `SmartDeskPower_Training.ipynb` in Google Colab (or locally via `python3 train.py`). The notebook:
1. Validates the threshold baseline by demonstrating the high false-trigger rate on `incidental`.
2. Trains the MLP on 33 features.
3. Generates the updated `SmartDeskPower_Pedrini_Bellini/model.h` file with weights and normalization constants.

### Phase 3: Demo Run
1. Open the serial monitor at 115200 baud.
2. Send `m` to start continuous monitoring.
3. **Quiet desk:** the state settles on `idle`; after 12 seconds the timeout fires: `POWER: OFF` (red LED).
4. **Start typing:** `typing` detected; after 2 consecutive windows `POWER: ON` fires (green LED).
5. **Writing with a pen / mouse use:** `writing` or `handling` detected, power stays at `POWER: ON`.
6. **Key moment — Incidental bump rejection:** stop and give the desk a sharp knock. The system classifies the window as `incidental`. The load **does not re-activate** (and the count toward activation does not reset).
7. After inactivity, `POWER: OFF` fires.
8. Send `s` to view the energy report and inference latency.
